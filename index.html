The Nurse and the Night Shift

Hollis Nwankwo has been a charge nurse in the same emergency department for eleven years. For the last three, a triage model has assigned every patient a number before they reach the desk. Hollis talks about what it is like to have an opinion about someone you have not met yet — and why, at two in the morning, giving that number up is unthinkable.

About the show

Most of what gets said about artificial intelligence is said by people with something to launch. Within Earshot is the other conversation: one guest at a time, someone who did not choose this technology but now works next to it every day. A nurse. A teacher. An interpreter. We ask what changed, in ordinary detail, and we stay long enough to get past the first answer.

Episodes run forty to sixty minutes and are edited lightly. Nothing is scripted. We keep the pauses in, because the pause is usually where the person is deciding whether to tell you the true version.

The rule

No guest is asked to predict the future. Everyone is asked what happened on a specific day. If an answer can be given at a conference, we have asked the wrong question.

Pitch a guest: someone whose job changed and who has not been asked about it.
